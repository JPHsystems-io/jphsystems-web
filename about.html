About

One engineer, accountable for the whole path.

JPHsystems is an independent practice covering cybersecurity, network security and software engineering. Those three are usually bought separately, and most of the expensive failures I have seen live in the gaps between them.

The Short Version

I like systems that behave the same way on their worst day.

My work sits between three teams that usually have different priorities: the people who build the application, the people who run the network, and whoever ends up holding the security risk. I have sat in all three seats, and the pattern holds: incidents are rarely caused by a missing product. They are caused by an assumption nobody wrote down.

So the practice is deliberately small. One engineer scopes the work, does the work and hands it over. That keeps the context intact and makes the accountability obvious. It also means I take a limited number of engagements at a time, and say no when the fit is wrong.

Principles

Five rules I do not trade away.

These are not values on a careers page. They decide what gets scoped, what gets refused and what the handover looks like.

  • Own the outcome. If I designed it, I answer for it after delivery. The 30-day question window is real, and so is the phone number.
  • Least privilege by default. Access is granted for a task and a duration, not for a person and a decade. The same applies to what I ask for.
  • Boring where it counts. Novelty belongs in prototypes. Authentication, backups and logging should be the least interesting parts of the system.
  • Write it down. A control that lives in one person’s head is a single point of failure. Diagrams, runbooks and decisions are part of the deliverable.
  • Leave the team able to run it. The engagement is not finished when the demo works. It is finished when your people can change it without me.

What This Is Not

A small practice, on purpose.

Knowing what a practice does not do is more useful than a list of everything it could. If you need one of the things below, I will tell you early and send you to the right kind of provider.

Not this

  • A 24/7 managed SOC
  • A body-shop for short-term staff
  • A hardware or licence reseller
  • A compliance certificate printer
  • A five-person agency with one engineer

This

  • Scoped assessments and builds
  • One named engineer, start to finish
  • Vendor-neutral recommendations
  • Evidence you can give an auditor
  • Written handover, every time

Remote

First, with recorded walkthroughs and shared screens. On-site by arrangement for physical changes.

1 day

Typical reply time to a new enquiry, from a person rather than a pipeline.

30 days

Of questions included after handover, because week one after delivery is when the real questions show up.
