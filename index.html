The Gap

The tools are the easy part.

You can buy a firewall, an EDR agent and a scanner by the end of the week. What you cannot buy is the policy behind them, someone who owns the alert, and the boring work of keeping all of it true six months later.

How I work
  • Flat networks. One compromised laptop can reach the database, the file server and the backup appliance, because nobody drew the line.
  • Standing access. Admin rights granted for one afternoon in 2022, still active, never reviewed.
  • Alerts nobody owns. A dashboard full of red that everyone has learned to ignore is worse than no dashboard.
  • Exposure without inventory. You cannot patch what you don’t know exists, and spreadsheets age faster than infrastructure.
  • Security as a PDF. A report with sixty findings and no owners is a transfer of liability, not a reduction of risk.

What 100% means

No orphan findings.

The deliverable is a list, not a document: every line has an owner, a fix and a way to prove the fix held. That is the difference between an assessment and something that sits on a shelf, and it is why the hand-off takes as long as it takes.

How an Engagement Runs

Short loops, visible work, no surprises.

  1. Scope

    A first exchange, then a written problem statement

    One message is usually enough to work out whether I’m the right person at all. If I’m not, I’ll say so and point you somewhere better. If I am, you get the problem written down in plain language before any money changes hands.

  2. Assess

    One to two weeks inside the estate

    I read the configs, the firewall rules, the cloud accounts and the logs. You get a findings document ranked by what it gives an attacker: where they get in, where they go next, and what it costs to stop them.

  3. Build

    Fixed scope, weekly demos

    We agree on a specific outcome and a date. Segmentation, identity, hardened apps, monitoring — built in your environment, in your repository, with your team watching. No black boxes, and nothing that only runs on my laptop.

  4. Hand off

    Runbooks, dashboards, and a team that can operate it

    The engagement ends when your people can change what I built without calling me. That means runbooks, a named owner for each alert and a recorded walkthrough. Ongoing support is available if you want it, but it is never assumed.
