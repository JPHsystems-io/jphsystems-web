Find the way in, close it, and prove it stayed closed.

02 / Network security

Assume the laptop is already compromised.

Most networks are one flat plane with a firewall at the edge and a VPN bolted onto it, which makes every compromised endpoint a route to everything else. Segmentation, least-privilege policy and monitoring keep one incident from becoming the whole story.

What I do

  • Network discovery and an as-built diagram
  • VLAN and zone segmentation by function and trust
  • Firewall rule review, clean-up and least privilege
  • VPN, ZTNA and remote-access design
  • IDS/IPS, DNS filtering and flow monitoring
  • Guest, Wi-Fi and backup-path isolation

What you get

  • A diagram that matches the equipment, not the invoice
  • A segmentation plan ordered by risk and effort
  • Implemented rules, tested before and after
  • Monitoring with a named owner for each alert class
  • Change log and rollback plan
  • Documentation your next engineer can read

At a glance

  • Best forOffice + cloud, flat VLANs, shared admin
  • Typical start1–3 week engagement
  • ThenMonitoring or quarterly review
  • DeliverableDiagram, policy, implementation
  • Works withExisting firewalls and switches

The Difference

The person who finds it is the person who fixes it.

Most security work is split across a reseller, an auditor and an internal team that inherits the consequences. That arrangement is efficient for invoicing and terrible for accountability.

The usual arrangement

  • A different account manager each quarter
  • Findings scored by tool severity
  • Licences proposed before diagnosis
  • Handover at delivery
  • Nobody to call when it breaks

This one

  • One engineer who did the work
  • Ranked by what an attacker can reach
  • Fix what you own before buying anything
  • Handover after your team can run it
  • A name and a number for 30 days

Questions

Before you get in touch.

Do you work remotely?

Yes, remote-first, with screen sharing and recorded walkthroughs. On-site work is possible by arrangement for physical network changes, hardware reviews or workshops. Travel is quoted separately and agreed in advance.

Can you sign an NDA?

Yes. I am happy to sign yours before any detail is shared, or provide a mutual agreement. Please do not send credentials, customer data or production secrets in a first message.

Do you resell hardware or licences?

No. There is no commission, no quota and no partner tier. If a product is the right answer, I will say so and help you buy it directly. If the gap can be closed with configuration you already own, that comes first.

How do you handle sensitive data?

Collect the least data needed, transfer it encrypted, keep it in an agreed location, and delete it at the end of the engagement. Testing is scoped in writing with rules of engagement, a named contact and a stop condition.

We already have an IT team or MSP. Does that conflict?

No. The usual pattern is that your team keeps running the estate and I take a specific system, assessment or build. I document what I change and hand it back, rather than creating a second shadow IT function.

What does it cost?

It depends on the scope, which is what the first exchange is for. After that you get a written fixed price and a list of what is included. No hourly surprises, no change orders for things that were obviously in scope.

Can you help during an active incident?

Possibly. Say so in the subject line and include the current state, what has been done and who is coordinating. If I cannot take it, I will tell you quickly rather than leave you waiting.
